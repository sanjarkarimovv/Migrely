Medication-overuse headache: the 10- and 15-day rules

Medication-overuse headache is headache on 15 or more days a month that comes from taking medicine for headache too often for more than 3 months: on 10 or more days a month for triptans, ergots, opioids, combination painkillers or a mix of kinds, or on 15 or more days a month for simple painkillers alone, one or several.

What is medication-overuse headache?

It is a headache disorder in its own right, in people who already have headaches such as migraine, and it was once called rebound headache. Medicine that eases an attack, taken on many days, can make headaches more frequent. It can come with chronic migraine, and a doctor may diagnose both.

How many days count as overuse?

The international classification (ICHD-3) counts days a month, kept up for more than 3 months:

ICHD-3: days a month, kept up for more than 3 months
Medicine for the attackDays a month
Triptans10 or more
Ergots (ergotamine)10 or more
Opioids, such as codeine10 or more
Combination painkillers: drugs of two or more kinds in one medicine, such as paracetamol with codeine, or a painkiller with caffeine10 or more
A mix of the kinds above, or of these and simple painkillers10 or more in total
Simple painkillers alone, one or several: paracetamol (acetaminophen), aspirin, ibuprofen and other NSAIDs15 or more
Any other medicine taken for attacks10 or more

Simple painkillers are one kind, so paracetamol on some days and ibuprofen on others still count toward 15, not 10.

Does it count doses or days?

Days. A day with two doses counts once. A day with a triptan and paracetamol counts for both kinds, and once in the total. Days you take these medicines for other pain, such as period or back pain, count too.

Are these safe limits?

No. They are the counts doctors use to diagnose it, not a safe amount. The NHS suggests not taking painkillers on more than 2 days a week, and NICE asks doctors to explain the risk to anyone using medicine for headache attacks.

How is it treated?

UK guidance (NICE) says doctors treat it by stopping the overused medicine for at least a month. Headaches are likely to get worse for a while before they improve, and there may be withdrawal symptoms, so doctors offer close follow-up and support, sometimes a preventive treatment, and a review 4 to 8 weeks after stopping. People who take strong opioids may need a specialist. ICHD-3 says it usually, but not always, gets better once the overuse stops.

Plan this with your doctor, especially if you take opioids, and don't stop or change medicines on your own.

When should I get help straight away?

Call your emergency number if a headache comes on suddenly and is extremely painful, or comes with weakness on one side, problems speaking or loss of vision. The NHS migraine page lists every warning sign.
