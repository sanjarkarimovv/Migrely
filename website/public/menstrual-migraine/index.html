Menstrual migraine: the day −2 to +3 window

Menstrual migraine means migraine attacks that happen from 2 days before a period to its 3rd day (days −2 to +3; there is no day 0) in at least 2 of 3 cycles. If attacks happen only then, it is called pure menstrual migraine. If they also happen at other times, it is menstrually-related migraine, which is far more common. A doctor confirms it, usually from a diary kept over a few cycles.

What is menstrual migraine?

It is migraine linked to periods. The international classification (ICHD-3) describes both kinds in its appendix, the criteria it uses mainly for research. The Migraine Trust says fewer than 1 in 100 women with migraine have the pure kind, while as many as two-thirds may have attacks linked to their period.

For this purpose, the bleed you get when you stop a combined pill or a cycle of hormone replacement therapy counts as a period too.

How do I count the days?

Day 1 is the first day of bleeding. The day before it is day −1, so there is no day 0. The window is five days:

How long should I track it?

UK guidance (NICE) suggests a headache diary for at least 2 menstrual cycles, and the Migraine Trust at least 3 months. Mark your period days and every attack. NICE suggests suspecting menstrual-related migraine when attacks happen mostly in this window in at least 2 of 3 cycles in a row. The free printable headache diary has a column for period days.

Does contraception matter?

It can. NICE advises doctors not to routinely offer combined hormonal contraception, such as the combined pill, for contraception to people who have migraine with aura. If you have aura, tell whoever prescribes your contraception.

How is it treated?

For period-related migraine that is predictable and does not respond well to usual acute treatment, NICE describes options a doctor may consider on the days migraine is expected. Talk to your doctor about what suits you.

Is my period data private in Migrely?

Migrely has no internet access and no account, so it cannot send your period days or migraines anywhere. They stay on your phone, in Android's own backup if you use it, and in any file you export. Tracking periods is optional, and you can check the privacy yourself.

Marking period days on the calendar is free. Once you have logged at least 2 cycles and 5 migraines, Insights, part of Premium, counts how many of your migraines started in these five days and shows how much of the time those days make up. It doesn't draw a conclusion: that is for you and your doctor.

When should I get help straight away?

Call your emergency number if a headache comes on suddenly and is extremely painful, or comes with weakness on one side, problems speaking or loss of vision. The NHS migraine page lists every warning sign.
