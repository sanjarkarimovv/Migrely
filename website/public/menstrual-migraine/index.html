Menstrual migraine: the day −2 to +3 window

Menstrual migraine means migraine attacks that happen from 2 days before a period to its 3rd day (days −2 to +3, where day 1 is the first day of bleeding and there is no day 0) in at least 2 of 3 cycles. If attacks happen only then, it is called pure menstrual migraine. If they also happen at other times, it is menstrually-related migraine, which is far more common. A doctor confirms it, usually from a diary kept over a few cycles.

What is menstrual migraine?

It is migraine linked to periods. The international classification (ICHD-3) describes both kinds, for migraine without aura, in its appendix of research criteria. The Migraine Trust says fewer than 1 in 100 women with migraine are thought to have the pure kind, while as many as two-thirds may have attacks linked to their period.

For this purpose, the withdrawal bleed in the break between packs of a combined pill, or during cyclical hormone replacement therapy (HRT), counts as a period too.

How do I count the days?

Day 1 is the first day of bleeding. The day before it is day −1, so there is no day 0. The window is five days:

How long should I track it?

UK guidance (NICE) suggests a headache diary for at least 2 menstrual cycles, and the Migraine Trust at least 3 months. Mark your period days and every attack. NICE suggests suspecting migraine linked to periods (it calls this menstrual-related migraine) when attacks happen mostly in this window in at least 2 of 3 cycles in a row. The NHS suggests seeing a GP if you regularly get attacks around your period. The free printable headache diary has a column for period days.

Does contraception matter?

It can. NICE advises doctors not to routinely offer combined hormonal contraception (the combined pill, patch or ring) for contraception to people who have migraine with aura, and the Migraine Trust says the same for other stroke risk factors. If you have aura, or get it for the first time while using one of these, tell whoever prescribes your contraception soon: they may suggest a different method. If you stop a method, it no longer protects you from pregnancy.

How is it treated?

Standard migraine medicines can be used for menstrual migraine, the Migraine Trust says. For period-related migraine that is predictable and does not respond well to usual acute treatment, NICE describes options a doctor may consider on the days migraine is expected. Days you take medicine for it count toward medication-overuse headache. Talk to your doctor about what suits you.

Is my period data private in Migrely?

Migrely has no internet access and no account, so it cannot send your period days or migraines anywhere. They are kept on your phone, and can leave it only in two ways: in Android's own backup to your Google account, if backup is on (end-to-end encrypted on Android 9 and later when your phone has a screen lock), and in any file you export and share. Tracking periods is optional: turning it off hides the marks, and any you saved stay until you delete them. You can check the privacy yourself.

Marking period days on the calendar is free. Once the months you look at include at least 2 period starts and 5 migraines, Insights, part of Premium, counts how many of your migraines started in these five days and shows how much of the time those days make up. It doesn't draw a conclusion: that is for you and your doctor.

When should I get help straight away?

Call your emergency number if a headache comes on suddenly and is extremely painful, or comes with weakness on one side, problems speaking or loss of vision. The NHS migraine page lists every warning sign.
