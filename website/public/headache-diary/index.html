Headache diary: what to write down, with a free printable template

A headache diary turns hazy weeks into facts your doctor can use. Here is what UK guidance suggests recording, for how long, and a free 8-week template to print.

Why keep a headache diary?

NICE, which writes guidance for doctors in England, suggests considering a headache diary to help diagnose headaches. A diary also shows whether a treatment is working, and gives you and your doctor something concrete to talk about.

What should I write down?

NICE suggests recording:

  • how often headaches come, how long they last and how bad they get;
  • other symptoms, such as nausea, or light or sound bothering you;
  • every medicine you take for them, prescribed and over the counter;
  • possible triggers;
  • any link to your period, if you have periods.

The template also asks whether each medicine helped.

How long should I keep it?

At least 8 weeks, NICE suggests. If headaches seem linked to your period, keep it for at least 2 menstrual cycles; the Migraine Trust suggests 3 months (more on menstrual migraine). For frequent headaches, the international classification (ICHD-3) says a day-by-day diary for at least a month is generally needed.

What counts as a headache day?

Any day with headache, for any part of the day (ICHD-3). Fill in a line on pain-free days too: they are part of the picture, and they make the count reliable.

Why count medicine days?

Taking medicine for attacks on many days a month can make headaches more frequent. This is called medication-overuse headache. ICHD-3 describes it in people who have headache on 15 or more days a month after more than 3 months of taking:

  • triptans, ergots, opioids or combination painkillers (such as those with caffeine or codeine) on 10 or more days a month;
  • a mix of these kinds, or of these and simple painkillers, on 10 or more days a month in total;
  • simple painkillers alone, such as paracetamol, aspirin or ibuprofen, one or several, on 15 or more days a month.

These are diagnostic thresholds, not safe limits: the NHS suggests not taking painkillers on more than 2 days a week. Don't stop or change medicines without talking to your doctor. More on medication-overuse headache.

When should I get help straight away?

Call your emergency number if a headache comes on suddenly and is extremely painful, or comes with weakness on one side, problems speaking or loss of vision. The NHS migraine page lists every warning sign.

What's in the template?

  • Page 1: how to use it, a key, and the warning signs that need urgent care.
  • Pages 2 to 5: two weeks each, one row a day, for 8 weeks.
  • Page 6: a summary for your doctor, with medicine days by kind.

It prints well in black and white, on A4 or US Letter.
